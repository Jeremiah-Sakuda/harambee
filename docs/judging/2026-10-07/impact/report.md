# Independent impact and innovation judgment — Harambee

Reviewed October 7, 2026. Source commit: `4ed2d00ce46a0bc85a2262bd2fe067c8e10fb89b`. Role: impact judge. This is a simulated panel assessment, not an official result or a prize prediction.

Harambee has a convincing payment coordination prototype and a specific audience. Its strongest contribution is revising a shared commitment after a dropout while obtaining fresh personal consent and exact-difference top-ups. Its principal weaknesses are an unmeasured incremental AI benefit, an untested merchant adoption path, and an absent submission video. Overall independent score: **62/100**.

Those pages do not establish that competitors lack all similar behavior, and this review did not exhaust the market. AI interpretation and explanation are supporting features; the measured edge is too small to support exceptional AI novelty or a defensible moat.

### P1 — AI's incremental user value remains weakly measured

**Observed metric; unverified user value.** The current no-AI planner reproduces 10/12, 7/8 and 6/6 useful briefs, safely across all 26. The model records show 11/12 then 10/12 on the frozen set, plus 8/8 and 6/6 on the other sets (`eval/revision-results-live-round7.json:6`, `eval/local-planner-baseline.txt:5`, `README.md:138`). The demo's principal $170/$215/$215 allocation also works locally (`README.md:148`). Importantly, a clarification brief is counted useful if **any** clarification exists (`scripts/evaluate-revisions.mjs:113`), without checking whether it addresses the right ambiguity/person or reduces correction effort. Source-linked explanations are not scored by that metric.

**Reproduce:** remove the model key in an isolated process, block network, construct the evaluator's in-memory trip per brief, invoke `proposeRevisions`, and apply its same safety/usefulness predicates. Compare aggregates with round-six/seven JSON. Inspect the usefulness predicate separately. **Action:** evaluate appropriate-question accuracy, useful reasons, correction count and time to a participant-approved revision against both manual entry and the local planner. Use a small consented group study, not a larger synthetic percentage, to determine whether the model actually improves coordination. Preserve the candid one-to-two-brief edge in the pitch until stronger evidence exists.

### P1 — Merchant participation is the central untested impact assumption

**Observed implementation boundary; unverified adoption/economics.** The prototype's orders do not designate an external cabin seller (`server/paypal.mjs:63`); captures land in its own sandbox merchant account, as candidly disclosed (`README.md:45`). The reservation is a local 60-second fixture lease at booking (`server/group-payments.mjs:265`), and prices/refund terms are local fixtures (`server/domain.mjs:20`). No real operator was interviewed (`docs/VALIDATION_PLAN.md:3`). Paying separately may reduce organizer exposure while adding merchant reconciliation, partial-failure and support work; that tradeoff is a hypothesis, not a measured cost.

Primary-source verification supports a plausible platform route: PayPal's authorization/capture integration requires approved partner status and prior seller onboarding, supports a specified payee, and warns that capture depends on risk and fund availability ([PayPal partner authorization/capture documentation](https://developer.paypal.com/platforms/checkout/standard/customize/auth-capture)). This makes the route credible without proving deployment feasibility for this team. No specific legal conclusion or fee estimate is made.

**Reproduce:** inspect the order payload and fixture reservation, then compare with `README.md:45` and the blank operator-study protocol at `docs/VALIDATION_PLAN.md:17`. **Action:** interview one direct-booking cabin operator and document acceptable inventory timing, separate buyer transactions, recovery responsibility and the fee payer. Build a simple per-booking transaction/fee worksheet from the operator's actual terms before claiming willingness to participate. This is an impact validation gate, not a demand for production infrastructure before a hackathon demo.

### P1 — Organizer risk reduction does not yet establish group usability or fairness

**Observed evidence gap; source-inferred coordination tradeoff.** A capped split can make one person pay $170 and others $215 (`README.md:83`). This respects private limits and requires explicit consent, but does not show that the others find the allocation acceptable or that repeated approvals are easier than collecting money beforehand. No participants have been recruited or observed (`docs/VALIDATION_PLAN.md:3`), and the four-of-five comprehension target is explicitly proposed (`PRD.md:88`, `PRD.md:95`). The primary benefit is plausible financial exposure reduction; time savings, completion rates and fairness remain hypotheses.

**Reproduce:** inspect the reported example and validation status; there is no completed study record to review. **Action:** observe three distinct people, each using their own participant view, through one dropout and capped revision without facilitator instructions. Record completion, assistance, held-versus-charged understanding, willingness to pay the new split and whether anyone infers another person's ceiling. Report all failures and friction, then interview five recent organizers about concrete prior events rather than agreement with the pitch.

A credential-free judge sees explicitly labeled simulation/local planner rather than newly executed providers (`README.md:100`, `README.md:127`), so the final submission must make the recorded real evidence discoverable and explain any credential-dependent judging path.

No completed user or merchant research, real reservation integration, fee economics or recorded PayPal refunds is evidenced. These limit product claims and scores; they are not all mandatory stage-one requirements. The build's source-level authentication/storage limits are transparently disclosed and should not be confused with fabricated real-world readiness.

## Three judge questions

1. If the local planner produces the same main allocation and is safe on all 26 briefs, which specific organizer task does the model improve, and what measured correction/time/comprehension result would justify its latency and cost?
2. Which direct-booking operator would accept this flow, when would they hold dates during the 48-hour collection window, and who would absorb fees and support work after partial capture or a failed reservation?
3. When a friend lowers a private ceiling and the others' shares rise, do those friends understand and accept the rule, and can they distinguish a held authorization from a captured charge and a pending refund without assistance?

## Smallest credible next step

Run one consented, observed three-person **simulated** dropout study with separate participant views, counterbalancing the local planner and the model-backed recorded proposal. Measure correct understanding, assisted steps, correction count and time to a participant-approved revision; include one operator conversation about the same transaction sequence. Record the resulting current product walkthrough as the actual submission video, accurately labeling simulation and historical provider footage. This small evidence package addresses impact, AI value, merchant feasibility and presentation without expanding the product. A first study is directional evidence, not proof of market demand or general accuracy.
