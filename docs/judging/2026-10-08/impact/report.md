# Harambee — independent impact and innovation assessment

Reviewed October 8, 2026. Commit: `0ce5ebecd2b93dfb7dad317c2332a3433f86f973`. Role: impact judge, independently scoring all five equally weighted criteria under `PANEL_RUBRIC.md`. This is a simulated panel assessment, not an official score or prize prediction.

Harambee demonstrates a useful payment agreement: a departure cannot silently increase anyone else's financial commitment, and a failed fixture booking can unwind separately authorized payments. The evidence supports that mechanism. It does not yet establish that groups finish bookings faster, chase people less, accept the allocation rule, or that cabin operators want the integration. The AI adds interpretation and supporting quotations, but the demonstrated allocation is predominantly deterministic.

**Innovation — 6.5.** The useful distinction is the integrated response to a departure: participant-confirmed limits, a new agreement, and additional authorization only for the difference, toward a merchant booking. This is a thoughtful combination rather than a novel payment primitive. Current primary sources narrow the claim further: [Wander](https://www.wander.com/article/introducing-split-with-friends-an-industry-first) already describes an organizer paying a share and a 48-hour group collection window; [TableCash's terms, sections 5.9–5.11](https://tablecash.app/terms) describe fixed-amount commitments, withdrawals before charging, recorded consent, group authorization before capture, cancellation refunds, AI planning and approval before an autopay increase. These sources do not establish the same merchant-directed difference-only hold revision, but they prevent crediting fresh financial consent or group commit/pay-later as exclusive ideas. The model's no-amount interpretation offers a plausible extra role; its comparative advantage over an ordinary participant form remains unmeasured. ## Three judge questions

1. What evidence would show that AI helps these groups reach agreement faster or with fewer corrections than directly asking each participant for attendance, cabin preference and a ceiling—and how will misleading summaries and unnecessary questions count against it?
2. Why would the first cabin operator accept multiple held shares for one stay, when are dates actually protected, and who pays for refunds or support if availability fails after the group commits?
3. Why would friends consider the capped-equal-share result fair, and does the promised benefit chiefly occur before booking, given that after booking the cabin cancellation policy applies?
